# Office Asset Management

## What's distinctive

Office is the most challenged asset class of the post-2020 cycle: a structural demand reset (hybrid work) layered on a cyclical capital markets disruption. Long-WALT leases delay the P&L impact of demand changes, sometimes by years.

## Key metrics

| Metric | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **WALT** | Weighted average lease term remaining (rent- or SF-weighted) | Single biggest valuation factor |
| **Direct vs. Sublease vacancy** | Available SF by source | Sublease overhang is a leading indicator of demand weakness |
| **Tenant retention rate** | % of expiring SF that renews | Office is expensive to release (TI / LC / free rent / downtime) |
| **TI / LC / Free Rent ("net effective")** | Concessions converted to effective $/SF | Captures true rent net of leasing costs |
| **Net Effective Rent (NER)** | (Total rent over term − concessions) / term | Apples-to-apples lease comparison |
| **Marked rent vs. in-place** | Gap between current market and expiring leases | Often negative in 2024-2026 office; means roll-down at renewal |
| **Operating Expense ratio** | OpEx ÷ EGI | Office tends to run 35-50%; rising as occupancy declines |

## Standard reporting cuts

- **Rent roll**: tenant name, SF, $/SF, expiration, escalations, options, credit
- **Top 10 tenants**: as % of total rent; flag any over 15%
- **Expiration ladder**: by year, by SF and by rent
- **Sublease exposure**: SF offered by current tenants for sublet; signals downsizing intent
- **Industry concentration**: legal / finance / tech / government / health — concentration risk
- **Class A vs. Class B vs. trophy comps**: flight-to-quality is the dominant theme; report relative to the right peer set

## Common business plans

- **Lobby / amenity reposition**: lobby refresh, fitness, conferencing center, food/beverage — $50-200/SF spend; targets rent premiums and retention
- **Spec suites**: pre-built turnkey small-tenant suites; lower TI ask from tenant, faster lease-up
- **Tenant credit upgrade**: roll out short-term tenants and replace with longer / better-credit tenants
- **Conversion** (residential or life sciences): increasingly common for B/C office in adaptive-reuse-friendly cities. Capital intensive and entitlement-sensitive.
- **Renew at all costs**: in weak markets, retention math often beats new-lease math because TI / LC for new tenants is 2-3x renewal cost

## Asset-class-specific risks

- **Tenant credit deterioration**: in any office, the rent is only as good as the tenant. Track tenant balance sheets and bankruptcies.
- **Capital reinvestment requirement**: office requires meaningful capex per lease (TI + LC). Underwriting that omits this overstates returns.
- **Lender appetite**: office financing markets remain difficult; refi risk is acute.
- **Hybrid work**: per-tenant SF utilization is falling 20-40%; tenants renew for less space at lease end.
- **Property tax / assessment battles**: declining values are creating successful appeals — both an opportunity (lower expenses) and a risk (declining comps).

## Quick performance diagnostic

For an office asset:

1. **WALT** — long WALT (>5 yrs) is shelter; short WALT (<3 yrs) is exposure
2. **Top-10 tenant credit** — any tenants on negative watch or in bankruptcy?
3. **Expirations next 24 months** — renewal probability × downtime × roll-down magnitude
4. **Sublease availability** in the building — even at 95% leased, 20% sublease availability = real economic vacancy
5. **Market direct vacancy** in the submarket — flight to quality means trophy assets are fine while B assets bleed

## Reporting one-liners

- "WALT extended from 4.2 to 5.1 years on the Smith renewal at $X/SF, accepting a 12% rent roll-down in exchange for term and avoiding ~$120/SF in re-tenanting costs"
- "Sublease availability in the building dropped from 8% to 3% as Acme returned 15k SF after renewing for 5 years at a smaller footprint"
- "Submarket Class A direct vacancy is 14% vs. our 8% direct vacancy — flight to quality is intact in this building"

# Infrastructure Asset Management

## What's distinctive

Infrastructure assets — utilities, transport, energy, digital — sit between real estate and operating businesses. They share long-duration capital intensity with real estate but operate under regulatory frameworks, concession agreements, or long-term contracts that govern returns. Asset management is heavily contract-driven, regulator-driven, or commodity-exposed depending on sub-sector.

## Sub-sectors and their economics

| Sub-sector | Return driver | Key risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Regulated utilities** (gas, electric, water) | Allowed return on rate base set by regulator | Regulatory risk; politically sensitive |
| **Contracted power** | Long-term offtake (PPA) at fixed price | Counterparty credit; basis risk |
| **Merchant power** | Wholesale electricity prices | Commodity volatility; weather |
| **Renewables** (solar, wind, storage) | PPA prices + Investment Tax Credit / PTC | Resource (wind/sun) variability; curtailment |
| **Midstream / pipelines** | Take-or-pay contracts; volumes | Counterparty; commodity for some types |
| **Transport** (toll roads, airports, ports) | Traffic/throughput × tariffs | Traffic volatility; concession terms |
| **Digital** (data centers, fiber, towers) | Contracted lease/colocation revenue | Tech obsolescence; tenant concentration |
| **Social infrastructure** (hospitals, schools) | Availability payments or rate revenue | Government counterparty; political |

## Key metrics

| Metric | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Contracted vs. Merchant** | % of revenue under fixed contract | Defines cash flow visibility |
| **WACT** (Weighted Average Contract Term) | Years remaining on contracts | Analog to WALT in real estate; bond-like value driver |
| **Availability** | % of time the asset is operational | Power, digital — must meet minimum thresholds |
| **Allowed Return / ROE** | Regulator-set return on equity | Regulated utilities only |
| **Rate base** | Approved invested capital subject to allowed return | Regulated utilities |
| **PPA price / Strike** | Contract revenue rate ($/MWh, etc.) | Power assets |
| **Throughput / Traffic / Load Factor** | Volume of service delivered | Transport, midstream, power |
| **Counterparty credit** | Rating(s) of offtake counterparty | Single biggest contract-value driver |
| **DSCR** | NOI ÷ Debt service | Project finance debt is highly covenant-driven |
| **CAFD** (Cash Available for Distribution) | After-debt, after-capex cash flow | What flows to equity |

## Reporting conventions

Infrastructure reporting often differs from real estate:

- **Operating P&L by asset** with availability, throughput, and CAFD
- **Contract status**: maturity, renewal probability, repricing exposure
- **Regulatory calendar**: rate cases, tariff filings, hearings, decisions
- **ESG / impact metrics**: greenhouse gas, water, safety incidents — increasingly required by LPs
- **Concession reporting** (where applicable): compliance, KPIs, hand-back conditions
- **Hedging position**: power assets often have hedge portfolios for commodity and basis risk

## Common business plans

- **Capacity expansion**: incremental investment to grow rate base (regulated) or add capacity (contracted)
- **Contract extension / repricing**: extend or refresh long-term contracts; biggest value lever
- **Operational efficiency**: heat rate improvement, throughput maximization, downtime reduction
- **Tariff escalation**: in regulated and contracted segments, indexed tariffs (CPI or fixed) drive growth
- **Counterparty diversification**: reduce concentration with new offtakers
- **Refinancing**: project finance debt often has scheduled refi events; rate optimization is recurring AM work

## Asset-class-specific risks

- **Regulatory**: rate case outcomes, allowed return reductions, retroactive disallowances. Single biggest risk in regulated.
- **Counterparty**: PPA buyers can become distressed (especially utilities, retailers in deregulated markets). Long-term value depends on counterparty staying current.
- **Commodity** (where unhedged): power, oil, gas exposures can swing revenue dramatically.
- **Curtailment / Congestion** (power, particularly renewables): grid constraints reduce realized prices below contract.
- **Concession risk**: government counterparties can renegotiate, expropriate, or alter terms (varies wildly by jurisdiction).
- **Technological displacement**: data centers obsolete on cooling generations; older fiber lit with old DWDM equipment; older gas-fired power assets stranded by renewables build-out.

## Quick performance diagnostic

1. **% of revenue contracted** vs. merchant
2. **WACT** and renewal cliff exposure
3. **Counterparty credit** quality and concentration
4. **Availability / utilization** vs. design
5. **Regulatory calendar**: pending rate cases or tariff filings
6. **CAFD** vs. target distribution

## Reporting one-liners

- "Asset is 92% contracted through 2032 (WACT 7.4 years) with weighted-average counterparty rating of A−; remaining 8% is merchant with $X.XM annual P&L sensitivity per $5/MWh"
- "Rate case decision expected Q3-2026; current filing requests $X.XM revenue requirement increase (ROE 9.8%); regulator's prior decisions suggest 60-80% capture probability"
- "Capacity factor of 38% vs. 41% P50 design; root cause analysis identifies inverter underperformance — $X.XM warranty claim filed and replacement scheduled"

## When in doubt

Infrastructure is heterogeneous. If you're unsure of the sub-sector, **ask the user** before underwriting analysis. The same word ("utility") can mean a regulated electric monopoly or an unregulated water company in a private deal. The right analysis differs entirely.

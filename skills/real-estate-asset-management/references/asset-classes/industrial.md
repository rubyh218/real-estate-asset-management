# Industrial / Logistics Asset Management

## What's distinctive

Industrial has been the strongest-performing major property type since 2017, driven by e-commerce penetration and supply-chain reshoring. The asset management story is usually about *capturing mark-to-market*: leases signed 5-10 years ago at half today's market rents.

## Key metrics

| Metric | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Clear height** | Interior ceiling height (ft) | 32'+ is modern; <24' obsolescent; drives tenant universe |
| **Dock door ratio** | Doors ÷ 10k SF | 1 per 10k SF = strong logistics; <1 per 25k SF = limited tenant base |
| **Trailer parking ratio** | Trailer spots ÷ 10k SF | Last-mile and distribution tenants need ample trailer storage |
| **Power capacity** | kW or amps per SF | Cold storage, manufacturing, EV-related demand higher |
| **Mark-to-market spread** | (Market rent − In-place) ÷ In-place | The headline value-creation lever in industrial |
| **NNN expense ratio** | What's recovered vs. paid | Almost everything is recoverable in industrial NNN leases |
| **Tenant credit** | Rated vs. private credit mix | Driver of cap rate compression |

## Standard reporting cuts

- **Rent roll**: tenant, SF, $/SF, lease expiration, contractual escalations, options
- **Mark-to-market exhibit**: in-place vs. market by tenant, with timing of roll
- **Expiration ladder**: typically more concentrated than office (fewer, larger tenants)
- **Tenant industries**: 3PL, e-commerce, manufacturing, distribution, cold storage — different credit profiles

## Common business plans

- **Roll-to-market**: hold through lease expirations to capture in-place to market gap (often 30-100% mark-up in 2022-2024 vintage acquisitions)
- **Trailer storage activation**: pave excess land for trailer storage at $50-150/spot/month
- **Power upgrade**: increase electrical capacity to attract cold storage, light manufacturing, or EV-related tenants
- **Cross-dock conversion**: convert single-load to cross-dock to attract regional distribution tenants
- **Building expansion / new building on excess land**: most industrial sites have 30-50% coverage; opportunity to add SF
- **ESG features**: solar (rooftop PV), EV charging — increasingly tenant requirement and value-add lever

## Asset-class-specific risks

- **Mark-to-market reversal**: a meaningful portion of post-2022 acquisition theses assume continued rent growth. Markets like LA, IE, and parts of NJ have seen 2024-2025 deceleration.
- **Functional obsolescence**: low-clear (<24'), small-bay (<50k SF), poor truck access — these properties face a shrinking tenant universe.
- **Tenant concentration**: most industrial buildings have 1-3 tenants. Single-tenant rolls are binary events.
- **New supply**: easy-to-build asset class. Tertiary markets have seen significant 2023-2025 deliveries.
- **Lease structures**: most industrial leases are NNN with full reimbursement, so opex is largely a pass-through — focus is on rent and occupancy, not opex management.

## Quick performance diagnostic

1. **Mark-to-market gap** — biggest value lever; quantify in $/SF and as a multiple of current NOI when rolled
2. **WALT and expiration timing** — when does mark-to-market unlock?
3. **Functional features** (clear height, dock doors, power) — define tenant universe at re-lease
4. **Submarket vacancy and net absorption** — market direction
5. **Tenant credit and renewal probability**

## Reporting one-liners

- "Building's $4.75/SF in-place rent reflects a 35% mark-to-market spread vs. comparable submarket pricing of $6.40/SF NNN; lease rolls in Q3-2027"
- "32' clear, ESFR sprinklers, and 130 trailer stalls on 18% site coverage position this asset in the top quartile of submarket inventory for last-mile users"
- "Submarket net absorption turned negative for 2 consecutive quarters; we are pulling renewal offers forward to mitigate re-lease risk on the Acme expiration"

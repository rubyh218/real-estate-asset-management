# Hospitality / Hotel Asset Management

## What's distinctive

Hotels are operating businesses, not real estate leases. There is no "tenant" — every night, every room re-prices. Revenue can move 30% in a quarter on demand, brand, and operating execution. Asset management is closer to managing an operating company than to running a long-WALT real estate portfolio.

## Key metrics

| Metric | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Occupancy** | Rooms sold ÷ Rooms available | Volume side of revenue |
| **ADR** (Average Daily Rate) | Room revenue ÷ Rooms sold | Price side of revenue |
| **RevPAR** | Room revenue ÷ Rooms available = Occupancy × ADR | The primary KPI |
| **RevPAR Index / RGI** | RevPAR ÷ Comp set RevPAR × 100 | Performance vs. competitive set; 100 = parity |
| **TRevPAR** | Total revenue ÷ Rooms available | Captures F&B, spa, parking, etc. |
| **GOP** (Gross Operating Profit) | Revenue − Department + Operating expenses (pre-fixed costs) | Operating leverage indicator |
| **GOP Margin** | GOP ÷ Total revenue | Cost discipline metric |
| **EBITDA** | GOP − Management fee − Property tax − Insurance − Reserves | What flows to equity |
| **Flow-through** | Δ GOP ÷ Δ Revenue | Captures operating leverage; healthy ~50% |
| **Penetration index** | Property RevPAR ÷ Market RevPAR | Underperforming or outperforming the market |

## Brand vs. flag economics

Most institutional hotels operate under a **brand flag** (Marriott, Hilton, Hyatt, IHG) via a franchise or management agreement.

- **Franchise**: owner controls operations; pays royalty (5-7% of room rev) + marketing (2-4%) + reservation/loyalty fees. Owner hires a third-party manager (Aimbridge, HEI, Pyramid, etc.) — or self-manages.
- **Managed**: brand operates the hotel directly. Base management fee (2-4% of revenue) + incentive (8-15% of GOP above threshold). Owner has less operational control.
- **Independent / soft brand**: more flexibility, less reservation distribution. Soft brands (Marriott's Autograph, Hilton's Curio, Hyatt's Unbound) split the difference.

The **HMA / Franchise agreement** governs PIP requirements (capex), brand standards, termination rights, and incentive fee thresholds. Read it.

## PIP (Property Improvement Plan)

Brands periodically require capital reinvestment to maintain brand standards. PIPs typically come at:

- **Franchise renewal** (every ~15-20 years)
- **Change of ownership** (acquisitions trigger PIP)
- **Brand standard updates** (often as the brand evolves)

PIP magnitude can be $20-100k/key depending on scope (renovation cycle: soft goods every 7 years, full case good replacement every 12-14). Major PIP requirements are often the trigger for sale.

## Standard reporting cuts

- **STR Report** (Smith Travel Research): industry-standard benchmark vs. comp set; tracks Occupancy, ADR, RevPAR Index by segment and by day-of-week
- **Segmentation**: transient, group, contract; leisure vs. corporate; OTA vs. direct vs. GDS
- **F&B performance** (full-service): banquet revenue, capture rate, F&B profit margin
- **Departmental P&L**: Rooms, F&B, Other Operated, A&G, Sales & Marketing, R&M, Utilities

## Common business plans

- **Brand repositioning**: change flag to a higher-RevPAR brand (e.g., Crowne Plaza → Hilton Garden Inn)
- **PIP-driven renovation**: spend through a PIP cycle to drive RevPAR Index upward
- **F&B repositioning**: restaurant concept refresh, banquet space upgrade
- **Sale of excess land**: many hotels sit on large parking lots; outparcels can be sold or developed
- **ADR push during demand strength**: discipline on ADR (not occupancy) drives flow-through

## Asset-class-specific risks

- **Cyclicality**: hotels are the most economically sensitive property type. RevPAR can drop 40-70% in a recession (COVID was −80% peak).
- **Operating leverage**: high fixed costs (labor, brand fees, utilities). Margin compresses sharply on revenue declines.
- **Labor**: largest single expense category. Tight labor markets and minimum wage changes drive material variance.
- **OTA dependency**: Booking.com, Expedia, Airbnb can take 15-25% commission. Direct booking share is a strategic metric.
- **Group / convention exposure**: in convention markets, single event cancellation can move a quarter.
- **CapEx intensity**: hotels require ongoing FF&E reserves (typically 4-6% of revenue) plus periodic PIP. Underwriting that omits this overstates cash.

## Quick performance diagnostic

1. **RevPAR Index trend** — outperforming/underperforming market
2. **ADR vs. occupancy mix** — is RevPAR growth from rate (good) or occupancy (limited room to run)?
3. **Flow-through** — is margin expanding with revenue or being eaten by expenses?
4. **STR competitive set drift** — is comp set still appropriate?
5. **PIP exposure** — what's the next capital reinvestment cycle and what's reserved?

## Reporting one-liners

- "RevPAR grew 8.2% YoY driven by 6.0% ADR and 2.1% occupancy; flow-through of 58% indicates strong operating discipline"
- "RGI of 104 (vs. comp set of 100) is up from 98 prior year following the November 2024 PIP completion"
- "PIP renewal in 2027 estimated at $42k/key ($14M total); FF&E reserves at $X.XM currently fund 60% of expected scope"
